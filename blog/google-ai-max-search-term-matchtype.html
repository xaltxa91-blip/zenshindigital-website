Google Ads {search_term_matchtype}: how to track AI Max expansion clicks

Google Ads added the {search_term_matchtype} ValueTrack parameter. Here's what each value means and how to separate AI Max expansion clicks from real exact, phrase and broad match clicks.

Key facts

  • {search_term_matchtype} records the match type of the search term that triggered your ad.
  • Values: e (exact), p (phrase), b (broad), a (AI Max).
  • When AI Max expands a keyword, {matchtype} still shows the keyword's match type, while {search_term_matchtype} shows “a”.
  • It can be used in final URLs, tracking templates and custom parameters.

Google Ads has added a new ValueTrack parameter, {search_term_matchtype}, to its help documentation. It records the match type of the search term that triggered your ad, and it makes it possible to see which clicks came from AI Max for Search expansions, even when they were credited to an exact or phrase match keyword.

The parameter was first reported on November 11, 2026 by PPC News Feed. Google has not published a separate announcement or a launch date.

{matchtype} vs. {search_term_matchtype}

Google's ValueTrack documentation defines the two parameters like this:

  • {matchtype}: “The match type of the keyword that triggered your ad.”
  • {search_term_matchtype}: “The match type of the search term that triggered your ad.”

Both return the same set of values:

Value{matchtype}{search_term_matchtype}
eExactExact
pPhrasePhrase
bBroadBroad
aAI Max (keywordless match)AI Max (expansions and keywordless matches)

Why the new parameter matters for AI Max

With AI Max for Search, Google can match a keyword to search terms beyond its own match type. In that case, {matchtype} still reports the keyword's match type, for example e for an exact match keyword. {search_term_matchtype} reports a, showing the click actually came from an AI Max expansion.

Combined with {targetid} (keywords start with “kwd”, AI Max keywordless matches with “kwl”) and {keyword} (empty for keywordless matches), the parameters separate three types of traffic:

Traffic type{matchtype}{search_term_matchtype}{keyword}
Standard keyword matche, p or be, p or bFilled
AI Max expansion of a keyworde, p or baFilled
AI Max keywordless matchaaEmpty

The scale of AI Max expansions is growing. According to Smarter Ecommerce data reported by PPC Land, the share of exact match impressions served through AI Max expansions rose from about 0% in January 2025 to nearly 29% by July 2026, across 383 million EMEA ecommerce Search impressions with AI Max active. With {matchtype} alone, all of those clicks would have looked like exact match.

How to set it up

  1. In Google Ads, open the tracking template at account, campaign or ad group level. ValueTrack parameters can also go in the final URL or in custom parameters.
  2. Add the parameters to your template. For example: 
    {lpurl}?keyword={keyword}&matchtype={matchtype}&search_term_matchtype={search_term_matchtype}
    Keep {lpurl} at the start. Google's AI Max guidance recommends this, because final URL expansion can send clicks to other pages of your site.
  3. Test the template with the “Test” option in Google Ads before saving.
  4. In your analytics or CRM, filter the URL parameters:
    • AI Max expansion traffic: matchtype is e, p or b, and search_term_matchtype is a.
    • Standard keyword traffic: search_term_matchtype is e, p or b.

AI Max for Search: timeline

  • May 2025: Google launches AI Max for Search campaigns, with search term matching, text customisation and final URL expansion.
  • July 2025: a “Search terms match type” segment is added to the Keywords report.
  • September 1, 2026: Google starts upgrading broad match and automatically created assets campaigns to AI Max for Search.
  • November 2026: {search_term_matchtype} appears in Google's ValueTrack documentation.

FAQ

What does “a” mean in {search_term_matchtype}?

“a” means the search term was matched by AI Max for Search, either as an expansion of one of your keywords or as a keywordless match.

What is the difference between {matchtype} and {search_term_matchtype}?

{matchtype} is the match type of your keyword. {search_term_matchtype} is the match type of the search term that actually triggered the ad. They differ when AI Max expands a keyword.

Where can I use {search_term_matchtype}?

Like other ValueTrack parameters, in the final URL, the tracking template or custom parameters.

Does {search_term_matchtype} work in Performance Max?

Google's help page does not list it for Performance Max. It is documented for Search campaigns, including AI Max for Search.
